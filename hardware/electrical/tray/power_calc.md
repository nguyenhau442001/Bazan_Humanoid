## UBECs

One 6 V 8 A UBEC per leg and a 6 V 3 A UBEC for the arms and neck: every branch has its own source, so a fault on one rail leaves the others up. A leg UBEC sees at most the 6.0 A firmware trip; sharing a leg UBEC with the arms left it under the 2 x derating. One UBEC for everything would see 8.7 A in the same fault, over its 8 A rating. Walking alone needs 3.20 A in all.

## Electrical safety: equal margins

Rule 2, selectivity: protection opens in layers, smallest and fastest first, so a fault takes out its own branch only: servo jam → firmware branch trip (6 A, 200 ms) → branch fuse → UBEC short-circuit limit → battery fuse (15 A) → BMS → cell CID. Fuse I²t: Littelfuse MINI 297 minimum melting values.

Rule 3, isolation: the 5 V buck for the Radxa and the STM32 (5 V logic → Schottky → Black Pill, mainboard/architecture.md) takes the battery directly, after the battery fuse and before any servo branch, so a dead servo rail leaves the controller alive to crouch the rest.

Start-up: with the soft start (one joint at a time) a UBEC sees 2.8 A; all servos at once would draw 13.4 A from each leg UBEC (burst rating 15 A) and 6.4 A from the arm UBEC (5 A): over the 6 A leg trip and the arm UBEC burst, so the soft start is mandatory.
